Urza's Oculus is an independent native macOS project for Magic: The Gathering Arena drafting. It is being built around a simple idea: useful recommendations should be explainable, grounded in evidence, and honest about what the data can and cannot say.

The current downloadable app is an early **Phase 0 ScreenCaptureKit prototype**. It can discover visible windows, let the player select a target, capture a still frame, and collect a bounded three-second frame sequence. Real-world capture validation is the next milestone.

OCR, card recognition, automatic pack tracking, persistent draft sessions, and live recommendations are not yet implemented. The project has independent foundations for card data, 17Lands metrics, draft events, reviewed archetype relationships, and a baseline evaluator, but these are not yet connected into a complete drafting workflow.

## The principles

### Your draft is the source of truth

The record of cards observed and choices made should drive the draft state. The drafted pool is derived from explicit, correctable event history rather than maintained as a separate, potentially conflicting source.

### Recommendations should explain themselves

A score without context is not enough. Oculus aims to show the evidence behind a recommendation, including relevant card performance, format context, data coverage, and uncertainty.

### Data should be reproducible

Results should identify their engine version, scoring configuration, schema, set, event, and dataset provenance. The same Swift recommendation core is intended to power the macOS app and generate public data artifacts.

### Local first, player controlled

The application is designed for local processing. It will not automate Arena interactions or make picks on the player's behalf. Captured screen contents are intended to remain on the device.

## Development roadmap

- **Phase 0 — Real-world capture validation:** test window capture and frame collection during a representative Arena draft, including scrolling and pack transitions.
- **Phase 1 — Recognition:** investigate local OCR and card identification against reviewed real captures.
- **Phase 2 — Draft tracking:** persist packs, observations, selections, corrections, and confidence.
- **Phase 3 — Recommendations:** connect the draft state to versioned data and explainable local scoring.
- **Phase 4 — Integrated companion:** validate the full capture-to-recommendation workflow with real drafts.
- **Phase 5 — Public data views:** publish selected, reproducible artifacts generated by the shared evaluator.

These are development goals, not claims that the features are already available. The current app is in Phase 0.

## Data and attribution

17Lands public datasets are an important source for draft and game statistics. Dataset availability, coverage, and metric definitions can change. Oculus preserves provenance and communicates known limitations rather than implying perfect parity or certainty. The current GIH baseline is copy-weighted and does not treat card copies as independent games.

Card metadata and imagery may use Scryfall services and data. Published Wizards archetype guides provide reviewed design-intent context; they do not establish draft pick order or card strength.
